Can the overlap-zone signals reach a 70% win rate and still make money?

The 70% TP-hit win rate is reachable with a quick first target, but those trades do not make money. None of the 38 changes tested beat the current defaults on unseen years, so the indicator's defaults stay as they are.

All numbers are in R: the result divided by the risk from the actual fill to the original stop. A trade counts as a TP-hit win when TP1 or TP2 is reached, even if the rest of the position later stops out. Cash-positive means the trade made money overall.

The four finalists

Development is 2015-09 to 2022, where the changes were designed and chosen. Holdout is 2023-01 to 2026-10-13, run once after the finalists were frozen. Fills are replayed on M1 with that minute's opening spread and 0.1 slippage. No swap or commission is included.

What the 50 charts and the numbers showed

Problem A
missed entries
Price turned within 0.10 ATR of the limit 19 times in 11 years: 13 would have won, 4 would have lost, and 2 never resolved. Placing the limit earlier catches these, but it also moves every other entry. With 0.05 to 0.25 ATR the results were the same or worse. With 0.5 ATR they looked better on 2015–2022 (+0.23R per trade) but came out at −0.02R on 2023–2026.
Problem B
almost at TP
Only 4% of losses reached 75% of the way to TP1, because TP1 is usually far away (median 3.9R). The charts showed three real habits. TP1 sits right at the opposite zone's edge. The TP1 rule skips nearer overlapped zones, where price often turned. And after TP1 the rest of the position often rides back to the original stop. The fixes for each of these did not hold up on unseen years: earlier targets, a TP1 at any nearer zone, a stop moved to entry after TP1, and a stop moved to TP1 after TP2. The stop to entry after TP1 came closest, at +0.08R on 2023–2026 against the baseline's +0.15R.
Problem C
losing trades
On the charts, losers often looked like a steady push or a spike through the zone, a stop taken by a wick just before the turn, or a news or weekend gap. In the numbers, none of these separated winners from losers. On 2015–2022, the chance of +1R before −1R stayed between 42% and 56% (noise level) for approach momentum, trend slope, distance from the SMA, zone size, age, strength, hour and waiting time. Wider and tighter stops both did worse.
Why the win rate
cannot simply rise
After the fill, price reached +0.3R before the stop 72% of the time (a random walk gives 77%), +1R 48% of the time (50%) and +3R 25% of the time (25%). The zone touch has no measurable direction edge, so a closer target raises the hit rate only by shrinking the payoff by the same amount. The baseline's small profit comes from rare far-zone winners, which average 3.9R.

Month by month, 2014 to 2026

"No data" months come before the Exness archive starts (2015-08-10) or after the cutoff; they are not zero-trade months. August 2015 is the engine's warm-up and is not evaluated. "R by entry" adds each trade's full result to the month it was entered. "R by exit" dates each partial exit by when it filled. Missed setups are orders that never filled although price came within 0.10 ATR of the limit, counted once per zone. Their columns show what would have hit first from that point: TP1, or the stop.

Every variant tested

All 39 runs (the baseline and 38 changes), sorted by their 2015–2022 result. Across them, the correlation between the 2015–2022 and 2023–2026 averages is only 0.2, so a good development result said little about the later years. Settings are in candidates_round1–3.json.

Walk-forward and costs

Each year from 2019, the variant with the best average R on all earlier years is used for that year only. The right-hand table repeats the finalists with spread and slippage doubled, and with the wider of each minute's opening and closing spread.

Data coverage. Exness XAUUSDm M1 bid and ask, 2015-08-10 to 2026-10-14 00:00 UTC. There is no verified data for 2014 or January–July 2015. The engine decides on 1H closes, the chart default. The shipped chart resolves fills on 1H candles; this study replays them on M1. In an independent check, 8 of 2,581 orders differed between the two.

Validation. Codex checked the data, the execution rules and the ledger arithmetic independently. Holdout years 2024–2026 had been looked at in an earlier session, so they are not fully unseen. The finalists were frozen before the holdout was run. One earlier freeze was withdrawn before any holdout number was read, after a bug (front-run orders re-placed every hour) was found and fixed.
